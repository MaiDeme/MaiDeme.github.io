Synthetic Data - Artificial Genomes

November 2023

Technologies: Python, GANs

For a few months in late 2023, I had the opportunity to work with a team of researchers in artificial genomes generated by neural networks. This is a brief overview of the project and my contributions.

Context

One of the main challenge of human genomics are the limited data availability due to privacy concerns and difficulties in obtaining large representative samples. Thanks to advances in generative models, it is now possible to create and use synthetics genomes that mimic real genomes properties. This approch has also the advantage of preserving individuals privacy since synthetic genomes do not correspond to real individuals. However, comprehensive methods to assess the practical usefulness of artificial genomes are still limited. In this project, we evaluate the utility of synthetic genomes through the lens of a population genetics task: local ancestry inference (LAI).

Local ancestry inference consists in identifying the ancestral origin of specific segments of an individual’s genome. This is particularly important for admixed populations, where individuals have genetic contributions from multiple ancestral groups.

Synthetic Genomes Generation

Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) were used to generate synthetic genomes. GANs consist of two neural networks, a generator and a discriminator, that are trained together in a competitive setting. The generator creates synthetic data, while the discriminator evaluates its authenticity compared to real data. Through this adversarial process, the generator learns to produce highly realistic synthetic genomes that capture the complex patterns and variations present in real genomic data.


Overview of the architecture of a GAN source

The data was already generated when I joined the team, so my main focus was on evaluating the quality and utility of these synthetic genomes.

Labeling Synthetic Genomes

The idea was to use synthetics genomes to train a LAI model but first we needed to label the synthetics genomes with a local ancestry. To do so, we used a KNN approach to assign local ancestries to synthetic genomes based on their similarity to real genomes with known ancestries. This involved calculating genetic distances between synthetic and real genomes and assigning the most probable ancestry based on the nearest neighbors.


KNN algorithm, green points are unlabelled one and read and blue are from the labeled dataset

We used a dataset composed of real genomes from fives superpopulations: African, East Asian, European, Soçuth Asian and American.

According to the labelling, American and South Asian were underrepresented in the synthetic data despite being balanced in the real data used to generate the synthetics genomes via the GANs.

Training neural networks for LAI with synthetic genomes

To assess the utility of synthetic genomes we trained a convolutional neural network called LAI-Net for local ancestry inference using the labeled synthetic genomes and compared its performance to a model trained on real genomes.


Table of results comparing LAI-Net performance when trained on real vs synthetic genomes

The results showed that the model trained on synthetic genomes achieved comparable accuracy to the model trained on real genomes except for AMR and SAS superpopulations.

One way of explaning this difference in performance is the high number of error in specific populations when using KNN for labeling. Testing on real genomes, KNN made significantly more errors for PUR (Puerto Rican), MXL (Mexican), ASW (African American) and CLM (Colombian) populations within the AMR superpopulations.

Number of KNN labeling errors per population when testing on real genomes

Conclusion

This project demonstrated the potential of synthetic genomes in downstream tasks such as local ancestry inference. Although challenges remain, particularly with admixed populations, synthetic genomes generated by GANs can serve as valuable training data for machine learning models in genomics. By generating diverse and representative synthetic datasets, we can enhance the performance of genomic analyses while addressing data scarcity and privacy concerns.
